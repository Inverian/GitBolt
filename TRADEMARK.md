# GitBolt Trademark Policy

## Overview

The name **"GitBolt"**, the **GitBolt logo**, and any associated visual identity elements (collectively, the "Marks") are trademarks of the GitBolt project and its copyright holders. This policy explains how the Marks may and may not be used.

The official GitBolt project is published at [www.gitbolt.com](https://www.gitbolt.com) and [github.com/ThePieMonster/GitBolt](https://github.com/ThePieMonster/GitBolt).

GitBolt's source code is licensed under the **GNU General Public License v3.0** (see [LICENSE](LICENSE)). The GPL grants you broad rights to use, modify, and redistribute the source code. **However, the GPL does not grant any rights to use the Marks.** Trademark law is separate from copyright law, and the Marks are protected independently of the source code license.

This dual approach — open source code with protected branding — is the same model used by:
- **Mozilla Firefox** (MPL code, "Firefox" trademark)
- **Visual Studio Code** (MIT code, "Visual Studio Code" trademark; the unbranded fork is called VSCodium)
- **Google Chrome / Chromium** (BSD code, "Chrome" trademark; Chromium is the unbranded build)
- **Krita** (GPL code, "Krita" trademark)

## What You CAN Do

You are welcome to:

1. **Use the source code** under the terms of the GPL-3.0 license, including modifying, redistributing, and creating derivative works
2. **Refer to GitBolt by name** in articles, blog posts, tutorials, reviews, comparisons, books, and educational materials
3. **State factual information** about GitBolt, such as compatibility ("Works with GitBolt") or interoperability ("GitBolt-compatible")
4. **Contribute to the official GitBolt project** through pull requests, issues, and discussions
5. **Distribute unmodified official binaries** that we provide, with the GitBolt name and logo intact
6. **Display screenshots** of GitBolt in articles, documentation, and reviews (so long as the screenshots are not altered to misrepresent the software)
7. **Use the name "GitBolt"** in your project name only when providing add-ons, extensions, or plugins that work *with* GitBolt (e.g., "GitBolt Statistics Plugin")

## What You CANNOT Do

You may not, without prior written permission:

1. **Use the GitBolt name, logo, or branding** for any forked, modified, or derivative version of GitBolt that you distribute. If you fork GitBolt, you must give your fork a **different name** and use a **different logo and branding**.
2. **Distribute modified builds** of GitBolt under the name "GitBolt", or any name confusingly similar (e.g., "GitBolt Pro", "GitBolt+", "GitBolt Enhanced", "GitBoltX", "MyGitBolt").
3. **Use the GitBolt name or logo** in a way that suggests official endorsement, sponsorship, or affiliation with the GitBolt project when no such relationship exists.
4. **Register domain names** containing "GitBolt" or confusingly similar variations.
5. **Use the GitBolt name or logo** in advertising, marketing, product names, company names, or commercial materials in a way that could create confusion with the official GitBolt project.
6. **Modify the GitBolt logo** or visual identity, including changes to colors, proportions, typography, or composition.
7. **Combine the GitBolt name or logo** with other names, logos, or branding in a way that suggests a partnership or joint product.
8. **Use the GitBolt name or logo** on merchandise, apparel, or physical goods for sale or distribution.

## Forks Must Rebrand

If you fork the GitBolt source code under the rights granted by the GPL-3.0 license, you are **required to rebrand** your fork. This means:

- Choose a different project name that is not confusingly similar to "GitBolt"
- Replace the GitBolt logo with your own original logo
- Update the application title, window names, About dialog, and all user-facing branding
- Update package names, bundle identifiers, and installer metadata
- Update repository names, documentation, and marketing materials

This is the same requirement that applies to forks of Firefox, Chrome, VS Code, and other GPL/MIT projects with protected branding.

## Why This Policy Exists

Open source licensing protects users' freedom to use and modify software. Trademark protection serves a different purpose: it protects users from confusion about the source and quality of software they download.

When users see the name "GitBolt" or the GitBolt logo, they should be confident that they are getting the official, supported version of the software — not a modified fork that may contain different features, bugs, or even malicious code. Trademark protection makes this trust possible.

This policy is **not** intended to discourage forks, modifications, or community contributions. The GPL-3.0 license fully permits all of these activities. This policy only ensures that the GitBolt name and brand remain associated with the official project.

## Requesting Permission

If you would like to use the GitBolt name or logo in a way not covered by the "What You CAN Do" section above, please contact the GitBolt project maintainers in writing. We are generally willing to grant permission for reasonable uses, particularly for educational, journalistic, or community purposes.

## Reporting Misuse

If you become aware of any use of the GitBolt name or logo that appears to violate this policy, please report it to the GitBolt project maintainers. We take trademark protection seriously and will take appropriate action to protect the integrity of the GitBolt brand.

## Enforcement

The GitBolt project reserves the right to enforce its trademark rights through legal action where necessary. We prefer to resolve disputes through friendly communication and will generally request voluntary compliance before pursuing formal enforcement.

## Updates to This Policy

This trademark policy may be updated from time to time. Material changes will be announced on the GitBolt project repository and website ([www.gitbolt.com](https://www.gitbolt.com)). Continued use of the GitBolt name or logo after such updates constitutes acceptance of the revised policy.
